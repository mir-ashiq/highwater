01 Watch the water

Gauge heights come from the U.S. Geological Survey and active alerts from the National Weather Service. Region watched: New Jersey & New York. When you’re offline, everything below falls back to the last snapshot — with its age shown, never hidden.

Checking the National Weather Service…
Find site numbers at waterdata.usgs.gov. Numbers are 8–15 digits. Your list is saved on this device, offline.

Gauge heights: U.S. Geological Survey Instantaneous Values service (public domain). Alerts: NWS api.weather.gov.

What this data can — and cannot — tell you
  • It can establish: the gage height each USGS sensor last reported, when that reading was measured, how much the level changed between two observations, and which NWS alerts were active for New Jersey & New York at the last verified check.
  • It cannot establish: whether your street will flood, any probability of flooding, conditions between gauge measurements, or conditions at places outside the watched gauges. Rivers can rise faster than sensors report.
  • When something is missing: the app shows the last verified reading with its age, or says it could not check. It never substitutes a guess, never shows a blank as “no alerts”.
  • Units & math: heights in feet as USGS reports them; a change is the plain difference between two readings over their exact time gap — not a forecast.

02 The 72-hour kit

The National Weather Service tells every household to keep enough food, water and medicine on hand at all times to last you at least 3 days — because oftentimes flash floods can occur within minutes and sometimes without any sign of rain. Check what you have. Your progress is saved offline on this device.

Drinking-water target — the arithmetic, shown

Ready.gov: Store at least 1 gallon of water per person per day for emergencies, and plan for at least 3 days. The number above is exactly that arithmetic — adjust for infants, illness, heat and pets. It says nothing about flood likelihood.

    03 The household plan

    NWS: create a communications plan with a specific person identified to contact for status updates or a safe location to meet up with family members — and don’t wait to be ordered to leave; evacuate yourself! Fill this in once, then print the pocket card in section 04.

    Neighbors to check on
    Do you know how to shut off…?

    NWS: clearly label circuit breakers, and if you evacuate with time to spare, disconnect utilities and appliances.

    NWS on insurance: Many flood insurance policies take at least 30 days to go into effect — so the right time to look into one is before the rain, not during. Standard homeowners insurance doesn’t cover flooding.

    04 Pocket card

    Print this, fold it, put it in a wallet and a go-bag. It carries everything that matters when the phones do what floods make phones do. Works from a dead network because it’s paper.

    F Field mode

    The phone-first view for when the power is out and the bars are gone: your people, your water, your essentials, and the saved readings — nothing else. Everything on this screen works from this device.
